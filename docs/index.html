Search intelligence research paper

Which content deserves a human refresh review first?

A time-aware ranking model for finding established content items whose search impressions are likely to decline over the next 28 days.

Hassan Raza Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring Public-safe summary

Abstract

This project asks which established content items an editor should review first when their search impressions may fall in the next 28 days. I built a page-level ranking model from completed search-performance windows and compared it with a transparent momentum rule. Candidate models were selected on May 2026 and evaluated once on a sealed June 2026 outcome cohort. The selected histogram gradient-boosting model achieved 0.65 precision@100 on the sealed cohort, compared with 0.38 for the fixed baseline. This is measured, directional decision support for human review, not a claim about Google's algorithm or evidence that refreshing content causes an improvement.

Introduction / problem statement

Prioritise scarce editorial attention

One row is one anonymised client-content item at a decision date. The output is a ranked private review queue. A wrong recommendation can waste time on a stable item, while a missed recommendation can leave a potentially important decline unreviewed. The model does not publish, rewrite, or change content automatically. It helps an editor decide where to look first.

Data

Completed windows only, with a sealed outcome month

The analysis uses the FlyRank internship warehouse table fact_content_daily_performance through DuckDB. It reads September 2025 to June 2026 partitions and includes stable clients with Search Console coverage from the beginning of the study. Two completed 28-day windows form the inputs. The next completed 28-day window forms the outcome proxy.

The proxy label is positive when next-window impressions are below 80% of current-window impressions. I excluded IDs, raw client or content identifiers, domains, URLs, titles, queries, GA4 engagement fields with uneven availability, and all future-window values. No raw warehouse rows or credentials appear in this public page or repository.

Methodology

A fair rule, then an honest model comparison

The baseline ranks items with falling recent impressions, weighted by current visibility, with a small extra weight for worsening average position. Logistic regression and histogram gradient boosting use the same seven eligible features: current impressions on a log scale, impression change, click change, current CTR, current average position, position change, and active days.

December 2025 through April 2026 cohorts train the candidates. May selects the method. June is sealed until the final measurement. The label and next-window values are forbidden model inputs. The random seed is fixed at 42.

The selected model improved sealed precision@100 by 0.27 over the baseline. Its ROC-AUC is modest, so the main use is prioritising a limited review queue, not making certain item-level predictions. The decline from validation to June is reported as uncertainty, not hidden.

Ranked recommendations

Use the score to start a human review, never to automate a change

  1. Start with the top 100 private scores. In the sealed measurement, 65% met the decline proxy, compared with 38% from the transparent baseline.
  2. Choose refresh review when an important visible item also shows negative recent search momentum.
  3. Choose search-intent review when average position worsens, after checking page and query context privately.
  4. Choose snippet review only after a visible, relevant item also shows low CTR.
  5. Choose monitoring for weak or conflicting signals.

The notebook produces an action_queue with priority rank, opportunity score, recommended action, and reason codes. It deliberately omits client and content identifiers from the displayed recommendation frame.

Limitations and honest framing

A ranking proxy, not a causal answer

The outcome is an impression-change proxy. Search demand, seasonality, indexing, measurement changes, and other unobserved factors can affect it. The evaluation cohorts are time-aware but not an intervention study. I therefore describe this work as observed, measured, directional, and useful for decision support. It does not prove that a refresh improves visibility or that the model captures Google's ranking rules.
